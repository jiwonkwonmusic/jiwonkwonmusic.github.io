About

Welcome! I am a PhD candidate in Music Studies (Ethnomusicology) at the University of Pennsylvania, currently in the fieldwork stage of my dissertation.

My work sits at the intersection of music, political power, and economic structures. Central to my research is an analysis of how the neoliberal logics of the state and the market affect musicians’ agency and labor, with a particular focus on creative autonomy, decision-making processes, and power dynamics with major industry actors such as record labels, streaming platforms, and digital distribution services.

By drawing on case studies in jazz, K-pop, and Western popular music, I explore the ways in which socio-economic hierarchies shape the production, circulation, and consumption of music in transnational contexts.

I received my M.A. in Music from Washington University in St. Louis and B.M. in Piano Performance and Jazz Composition from Berklee College of Music. I am an active pianist and composer specializing in jazz and pop, and years spent playing and writing music continue to shape the questions I bring to my research today.
